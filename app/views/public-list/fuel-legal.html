Legal basis for solid fuel certification

From: Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs

Last updated 14 January 2026

Certified solid fuels can be burned in uncertified appliances in smoke control areas.

Fuels that are on the list of certified fuels are legally certified for use in at least one UK country. Not all fuels on the list are certified for use in all UK countries. Check the specific country certifications of each fuel.

Fuels that aren't on the list aren't certified for use in smoke control areas anywhere in the UK.

Country certification

Fuel certification in each UK country is under the authority of that country's government.

England

Fuels for use in England are certified by the Secretary of State for the Environment.

Fuels certified for use in smoke control areas in England are certified under Sections 20 and 21 of the Clean Air Act 1993, as updated by Section 15 of the Deregulation Act 2015.

Fuels certified for domestic use in England are certified under the Air Quality (Domestic Solid Fuels Standards) (England) Regulations 2020.

Scotland

Fuels certified for use in smoke control areas in Scotland are authorised by Scottish Ministers.

Fuel certification for Scotland is under Sections 20 and 21 of the Clean Air Act 1993, as updated by Section 50 of the Regulatory Reform (Scotland) Act 2014.

Wales

Fuels certified for use in smoke control areas in Wales are authorised by Welsh Ministers.

Fuel certification for Wales is under specific regulations made by Welsh Ministers under the Clean Air Act 1993.

Northern Ireland

Fuels certified for use in smoke control areas in Northern Ireland are authorised by the Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs.

Fuel certification for Northern Ireland is under Article 17(7) of the Clean Air (Northern Ireland) Order 1981, as updated by Section 16 of the Environmental Better Regulation Act (Northern Ireland) 2016.

Fuels that are no longer commercially available

Fuels that are no longer commercially available are still certified if they:

  • are used in England outside of smoke control areas, and
  • are on the list of certified fuels, and
  • were manufactured before the dates specified in the relevant legislation
