About solid fuel certification

From: Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs

Last updated 14 January 2026

Fuel certification is decided separately by each UK country. Even if a fuel is on the list of certified fuels, you must check that:

  • the fuel is certified in the country where you'll use it
  • your appliance is designed to burn that type of fuel

If a fuel isn't included on the list of certified fuels, it's not a certified fuel.

Fuel status and meaning

Certified

Certified fuels can be burned in that country's smoke control areas.

Fuels certified in England can also be burned in homes (domestic settings) anywhere in England under the Domestic Solid Fuels etc.

Uncertified

An uncertified fuel has either been rejected for certification in that country or that country hasn't certified it yet.

You can only burn an uncertified fuel in a smoke control area in an appliance that:

  • is on the list of certified appliances, and
  • is certified to burn that type of fuel

Non-domestic

England only

Non-domestic fuels are only certified for use in non-domestic settings in England, such as restaurants, pubs and industry.

They aren't certified for home (domestic) use anywhere in England.

Last certified dates

In England

Fuels are recertified every year in England, if they still meet all the requirements for certification. For England, the date the fuel was last certified is either:

  • when it was most recently recertified (or when it was first certified, if that was less than a year ago), or
  • the last day before its certification was revoked

In Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland do not recertify fuels. For these countries, the date the fuel was last certified is either:

  • when it was first certified, or
  • the last day before its certification was revoked

Relevant legislation

Fuel certification in each UK country is under the authority of that country's government.

England

The list of certified fuels for England is published by the Secretary of State for the Environment. Fuels included on the list are “approved fuels” for the purposes of the Clean Air Act 1993 (in particular Sections 19A to 19D).

Fuels included on the list are also “authorised fuels”, in accordance with The Air Quality (Domestic Solid Fuels Standards) (England) Regulations 2020 (in particular Regulations 11 and 12). These fuels are certified for domestic use anywhere in England.

Scotland

For smoke control areas in Scotland, fuels included on the list of certified fuels are certified by Scottish Ministers under Sections 20 and 21 of the Clean Air Act 1993.

Wales

The list of certified fuels for Wales is published by Welsh Ministers. Fuels included on the list are “authorised fuels” for the purposes of the Clean Air Act 1993 (in particular Sections 19A to 19D).

Northern Ireland

For smoke control areas in Northern Ireland, fuels included on the list of certified fuels are certified by the Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs under Article 17(7) of the Clean Air (Northern Ireland) Order 1981.
