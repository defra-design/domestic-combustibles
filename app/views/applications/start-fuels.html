Get a solid fuel certified for use in smoke control areas

Contents

  1. Application fees
  2. Requirements for all applicants
  3. Requirements for fuel manufacturers
  4. Requirements for rebranded fuel

Use this service to apply to get a solid fuel certified for use in domestic premises or smoke control areas in England.

You must fill out a separate application for each different fuel you want to get certified.

You'll need to provide information about your company and about the fuel, including brand information.

Manufacturers will also need to provide:

  • fuel details such as its appearance, weight and composition
  • details of the manufacturing process
  • test reports showing that the fuel meets legal emissions limits

For rebranding, you’ll need to provide:

  • identifying information about the original certified fuel
  • a letter of permission from the manufacturer to rebrand their fuel

You should review the Ready to Burn fuel certification terms and conditions before starting your application.

Application fees

It costs £950 (excluding VAT) for a manufacturer to get an original fuel certified.

It costs £475 (excluding VAT) to get a rebranded fuel certified.

The certification organisation will request fees once they have received your application.

Requirements for all applicants

Company information

You'll need to provide the following information about your business:

  • company name and address
  • whether you have a system in place to deal with customer complaints
  • how you sell the fuel (loose, bagged at source or bagged after being transported somewhere else)

You'll need to tell us whether you:

  • manufacture the fuel
  • rebrand fuel made by someone else

This information is required for your application only, and will not be made public.

You also have the option to provide other documents to support your application, for example:

  • information about your quality control processes
  • product packaging information

Company contacts

You'll need to give us:

  • the name and email address of a person we can contact if we have questions about your application
  • the name of the person responsible for the fuel's certification, which will appear on the fuel's certificate

These might both be the same person.

Requirements for fuel manufacturers

You'll need to provide test reports proving that the fuel meets legal emissions limits.

You'll also need to provide details of the fuel:

  • its shape, size and any recognisable markings
  • information about the fuel's weight
  • the fuel's exact composition and sulphur content
  • the manufacturing process used

And you'll need to tell us:

  • whether you have a monitored quality control system in place
  • what brand names you'll sell the fuel under

Test reports

As the fuel manufacturer, you must provide test reports that show that the fuel meets legal limits for:

  • smoke emissions (less than 5g per hour)
  • sulphur content (less than 2%)

Additional tests may be required in the case of complaints or non-compliance.

Testing must be done by a laboratory accredited to BS 3841. If we don't recognise the testing laboratory, we might ask you for clarification.

You can find more detailed information about testing requirements in the Ready to Burn fuel certification terms and conditions.

Fuel appearance

You will need to explain the specific shape, size and any recognisable markings on the fuel.

For example:

  • Pillow-shaped briquettes with a single line indentation on one side and a double line indentation on the reverse side
  • Firelogs that are approximately 225 millimetres in length and 70 millimetres in depth, with grooves along their sides
  • Unmarked charcoal pieces between 40 and 110 millimetres in length

Fuel weight

You will need to tell us the average weight of the fuel.

For example:

  • Average weight of 125 to 135 grams per briquette
  • Average weight of 0.70 kilograms per firelog
  • Average weight of 54 to 56 grams per large briquette or 26 to 28 grams per small briquette

Fuel composition

You will need to list everything the fuel is made of, and approximately what percentage each part is of the total weight.

For example: The fuel contains anthracite fines (60% to 80%), petroleum coke (10% to 30%), bituminous coal (0% to 17%) and molasses/acid or an organic binder (remainder).

Manufacturing process

You will need to briefly explain the exact methods and temperatures used to manufacture the fuel.

For example: The fuel is manufactured by a process involving:

  • roll-pressing and heat treatment at about 300°C
  • roll-pressing and cold curing at ambient temperature
  • kiln pyrolysis at approximately 500 to 600°C
  • heat treatment and extrusion

Sulphur content

You will need to provide the sulphur content as an approximate percentage of the total dry, ash-free weight of the fuel. For example, 1.9% or 0.05%.

Requirements for rebranded fuel

For rebranded fuels, you must provide:

  • the name of the original fuel manufacturer
  • the name or brand of the original fuel
  • a letter from the original manufacturer proving that you have permission to rebrand their fuel

This information is required for your application only and will not be made public.

You will also need to tell us:

  • whether the original fuel has been changed or mixed with another fuel
  • what brand names you will be selling the fuel under
